Lesson 8 · 16 min

Terrain curriculum and your training plan

Nobody trains stair walking on day one: the terrain and the requested speed grow as the policy succeeds. In this lesson you measure how far up the ladder the ready-made policy goes, read how mjlab and unitree_rl_lab level up, and write your own training plan in a file that a script checks.

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  1. 1Steps of 2 and 3 cm pass; from 4 cm up, it falls
  2. 2Curriculum: it moves up if it walked more than 80% of the request, down if it walked less than 50%
  3. 3Command curriculum: the speed range opens 0.1 m/s at a time
  4. 4The plano-treino.md with robot, command, reward, curriculum and evaluation

Goal: measure the ready-made policy's maximum step, read the curriculum rules, and write a training plan with 5 fields that conferir_plano.py approves.

02 · How far up the steps the ready-made policy goes

terreno.py uses MjSpec to build a platform of height h 1.5 m from the robot and tells it to walk for 8 s at 0.5 m/s. Passed = it did not fall and the pelvis ended up on top (x > 2.5 m).

Terminal · five steps
F=${FTH:-$HOME/fth}; cd $F/locomocao && . $F/.venv/bin/activate
python3 kit/terreno.py --degraus 0.02 0.03 0.04 0.06 0.10; echo "saída: $?"

2 and 3 cm pass; from 4 cm up, the robot falls at x ≈ 2 m, before getting onto the platform. The steps that fail are the next levels of a curriculum: that is what you retrain for.

03 · How the repositories move up a level

Two curricula run together in real training. Click each one.

1. Terrain

In mjlab, at the end of each episode, a robot that walked more than 80% of the requested distance moves up one terrain level; one that walked less than 50% moves down. Each robot has its own level: in the same training run, some are on flat ground and others on pebbles.

2. Command

In unitree_rl_lab, the requested velocity starts at ±0.1 m/s. When velocity tracking passes 80% of the maximum, the range opens 0.1 m/s on each side, up to the limit of −0.5 to 1.0 m/s.

3. Push

The push is not part of the curriculum in these two repositories: ±0.5 m/s in x and y, every 5 s in unitree_rl_lab; in mjlab, every 1 to 3 s, with ±0.4 m/s in z as well and roll, pitch, and yaw rotations.

4. Flat

Before spending GPU: which robot, what command range, which terms and weights, what terrain levels, and which test approves. It is the file you write now.

04 · Your training plan

Copy the template to ~/fth/treino, replace each [bracket] with your value, and check it. The kit example shows a plan that passes.

File · kit/plano-treino-modelo.md
# Plano de treino de locomoçêo

Robô: [qual humanoide e qual arquivo, ex.: Unitree G1 29 GDL, unitree_g1/scene.xml do Menagerie 62ab00f]
Comando: [faixa de velocidade, ex.: vx de -0.5 a 1.0 m/s, vy de -0.3 a 0.3 m/s, giro de -0.2 a 0.2 rad/s]
Recompensa: [termos e pesos, ex.: track_linear_velocity 2.0, upright 1.0, action_rate_l2 -0.1]
Currículo: [níveis em ordem, separados por →, ex.: plano → degrau de 5 cm → rampa → pedriscos]
Avaliaçêo: [critério de aprovaçêo com número, ex.: 18/20 episódios de pé com empurrêo de 1.0 m/s]
Terminal · copy and check
F=${FTH:-$HOME/fth}; cd $F/locomocao && . $F/.venv/bin/activate
[ -f $F/treino/plano-treino.md ] || cp kit/plano-treino-modelo.md $F/treino/plano-treino.md
python3 kit/conferir_plano.py $F/treino/plano-treino.md; echo "saída: $?"
