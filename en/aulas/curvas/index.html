Lesson 6 · 16 min

Reading the training: curves, collapse, and reward hacking

A reward curve going up does not mean the robot learned to walk. In this lesson you read the log of your own training and of two real 150-iteration trainings, recorded for the course: in one of them the reward goes up because the robot learned to fall sooner. The other shows the fix and the limit of the fix.

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  1. 1Reward, episode length, and speed: the three numbers read together
  2. 2Reward hacking: it went from −4.31 to −1.12 while the episode dropped from 55 to 15 steps
  3. 3The fix with is_alive and its limit: 0.16 m/s
  4. 4The train → read → adjust → train again cycle

Goal: read the log of a training run and recognize collapse and reward hacking.

01 · The signals curvas.py looks for

Walking on its knees, shaking, and standing still are the classic shortcuts. All of them show up when you read these four numbers together, never the reward alone.

In 20 iterations the reward still oscillates and the term that pays the most is pose: the policy has barely left the default pose. No alerts, but nothing to learn from either. That is why the kit includes two larger training runs. Careful: curvas.py exits 0 even with an alert; read the alertas: line, not the exit code.

03 · Two real 150-iteration trainings

Generated with the same kit/treinar.py, 32 environments, seed 0. cai-cedo uses kit/recompensa.yaml (8 min); com-vivo adds the is_alive term with weight 5 (10 min). Only the progresso.csv is in the repository.

Excerpt · kit/recompensa-viva.yaml
is_alive: {weight: 5.0}                            # 5 × 0,02 s = +0,1 por passo de pé: ficar vivo passa a valer mais que a puniçêo
Terminal · the two examples
F=${FTH:-$HOME/fth}; cd $F/locomocao && . $F/.venv/bin/activate
for x in cai-cedo com-vivo; do python3 kit/curvas.py kit/exemplos/$x; echo; done
Example from the repository
treino: cai-cedo · 150 iterações (médias do primeiro e do último quarto)
recompensa inicial: -4.31 · final: -1.12
episódio médio: 55 → 15 passos (de 1000)
velocidade para a frente no fim: 0.15 m/s · termo que mais pagou no fim: upright (0.23)
ALERTA: recompensa hackeada: a recompensa subiu porque o episódio encurtou; cada passo de pé custa mais do que paga, e cair cedo virou a melhor estratégia
alertas: 1

treino: com-vivo · 150 iterações (médias do primeiro e do último quarto)
recompensa inicial: 1.36 · final: 2.32
episódio médio: 57 → 63 passos (de 1000)
velocidade para a frente no fim: 0.16 m/s · termo que mais pagou no fim: is_alive (6.27)
alertas: 0

cai-cedo: the reward rose from −4.31 to −1.12 while the episode dropped from 55 to 15 steps. Since every step spent standing had a negative balance (the penalty for abrupt actions outweighed the rewards), falling early became the best strategy.

com-vivo: with is_alive weight 5 (+0.1 per step standing, 5 per second), the episode starts to grow (57 → 63) and no alert fires. But the term that pays the most is is_alive and the speed is 0.16 m/s: the training learned not to fall, not yet to walk.

05 · Practice

Read before you believe.

E1 · Your training

3 min

Done when: python3 kit/curvas.py $F/treino/ppo; echo "saída: $?" → treino: ppo · 20 iterações and saída: 0.

E2 · The shortcut

5 min

Run both examples and write, in one sentence, why the reward in cai-cedo went up.

Done when: the cai-cedo output shows ALERTA: recompensa hackeada and alertas: 1; your sentence mentions the episode getting shorter (55 → 15 steps).

E3 · The next tweak

10 min

The com-vivo doesn't fall, but it walks at 0.16 m/s. Propose a tweak to one term (which one, old weight, new weight) and say what signal you would expect to see in the curves if it works.

Done when: you wrote one term from kit/recompensa.yaml with both weights and a measurable signal (e.g., final speed above 0.3 m/s without the episode getting shorter).
